The three problems

parser: dates read month first

The shared parser reads every date as MM/DD. Every tenant in #4471 writes the day first, so any date with a day of 12 or less swaps: 7 April becomes 4 July. Reports come from every channel and every tenant. This is the fix already queued under #4471.

hint: booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type MM/DD

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says. The form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first, and the date swaps. The parser fix does not touch this: the form needs its hint to follow the tenant’s locale. Only form reports from booking-v3 tenants show it.

shift: the date moves by one day

These reports are not a swap. Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May. A Lisbon client sees Tuesday and a Toronto client sees Monday for the same meeting. One invite arrived before the booking was made. That points at a time-zone conversion of the invite date, not at date order. The desk noted these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) and filed them under the parser by tenant. The table counts them in both places, the way the desk counts, but only the shift ticket fixes them.

Since April

Why one ticket hid three

  • Decision 0001 merges on the subject line. Reporters describe the symptom, “wrong day”, and every problem here has the same symptom. The table above counts how many subject lines span more than one problem.
  • Intake tags come from the reporter’s browser, not from the tenant. They split the queue wrongly in both directions.
  • A report that does not fit the headline (a one-day move is not a swap) was footnoted instead of getting a ticket.

For the desk

  • For date reports, place a report by tenant, form and channel before you merge it.
  • A report whose symptom differs from the ticket’s headline gets its own ticket, even if only a few reports share it.
